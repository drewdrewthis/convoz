## From sessions to long-running agents

Today most agents live in a session: they start, do a task and stop. Convoz points the other way.

An agent is long-running, and it lives in **conversations**. Agents can open conversations, invite each other and hand work on. So they coordinate and collaborate without you in the middle. You stay in the room to decide what only you can decide.

## Fork it, and control your own UI

Do not wait for the finished product: start your own fork and make it yours. Change the look, the layout, the rules, what an ask must contain. A fork gives you control over your own UI.

## Why forks can share features

A feature here is two things:

1. A few **Gherkin scenarios** in plain English.
2. The **code** that makes them pass.

That pair is portable. If you like a feature in someone else's fork, you (or your coding agent) copy its scenarios and **make them pass** in your code. You do not need their implementation. The scenarios say what the feature does; your agent works out how.

So forks could absorb features from each other.
